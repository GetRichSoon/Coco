## 2. Ranked weaknesses (most severe first)

1. **Unsupported or contradictory exam claims inside the book** (risk: credibility and a "no claim about pass rates" promise the book itself breaks). Detail in section 3. Fix: remove or source the quarter-failing sentence; add the optional 10-minute break; soften "the book names the source each time".
2. **Positioning mismatch.** The body speaks to someone "who has just sat the Clinical examination and not passed" (chapter 1 first lines, chapter 6, chapter 24), but the title, cover and thumbnail speak to every candidate and the cover never says LCSW or ASWB. First-time candidates (the larger search audience) may feel it is not for them; retakers do not see themselves on the cover. Fix: choose one (recommended: lead with the retake plan as the sharper differentiator, and add a short "first attempt? adapt it like this" note), and put the audience on cover and title.
3. **Cover and thumbnail do not match category conventions.** Competitors use bold colour, badges ("2026-2027 Edition", "200 Practice Questions"), and mockups. Ours is quiet navy with tiny text. At thumbnail size the only words are "90-DAY CLINICAL LICENSING EXAM STUDY PLAN"; no "LCSW". Fix: see COVER_CONVENTIONS.md.
4. **No ready-to-use tools.** The pitch is "a plan on a calendar", yet the buyer receives tables to re-draw. Competitors include 5-page printable planners (free) or a Notion template ($9). Fix: add an appendix (blank 13-week calendar grid, scorecard, coverage map, question template, exam-week logistics page). All could reuse tables the book already contains.
5. **Format and purchase experience.** EPUB only, no PDF; no cover inside the EPUB; copyright and "trademarks and independence" at the end only. Many exam-prep buyers expect PDF (Test Treasure and Pass With Ayo both advertise PDFs with page counts). Fix: add a PDF edition built from the same text (owner decision), keep EPUB as bonus.
6. **Editing inconsistencies** (all low-risk but visible; see section 4).
7. **Gumroad page gaps**: empty summary, one-paragraph description, no refund wording, no FAQ, no preview, no page count.
8. **SEO**: title lacks search terms; tags miss "lcsw study plan"/"aswb clinical"/"retake".

## 4. Manuscript quality (concrete findings)

Strengths: clear single method; honest about not teaching clinical content; good "if you are not ready" guidance; sensible compressed-plan rules; arithmetic checked (pacing checkpoints, worked-example gap rates, 240/122 = 118 seconds).

Gaps and defects:
- **Completeness / tools**: no blank calendar, scorecard, coverage-map grid, question template, logistics page, or day-by-day table for phase 2 beyond block counts per week. A "90-day plan" reader expects to see day 1 to day 90.
- **Examples**: three scenarios only (all in chapter 19); no worked example of a statement filled in four parts; no sample completed scorecard row for the first review.
- **Depth on accuracy risk**: scenario 1 (possible suicidal ideation) is correctly labelled as a method demonstration, but the author states the book is not clinical authority. Consider adding "confirm in your references" at that scenario, not only at the chapter start.
- **Inconsistencies**: "twelve weeks" used in many places while the plan is 90 days (about 13 weeks); phase 3 says "two or three full-length rehearsals" and "done it four times" while the schedule has two half-length sessions, the ASWB practice test and one full-length session; chapter 14 says no new material after day 60, chapter 16 says no new statements after day 70; chapter 1 calls Part V "leave it until week ten" while chapter 3 says read chapter 22 in week one if eligible for a waiver.
- **Typos / awkward lines**: chapter 7 "Note day 1's placement of ethics on day 2"; chapter 18 "is, a way of converting right answers into wrong ones"; several lines in chapters 5 and 15 have uneven line breaks where a sentence was clearly inserted later.
- **Repetition**: chapters 25 and 26 overlap (closing note, "where to go next", last word); chapters 27 and 28 repeat the "we do not teach clinical content" statement already made twice in chapter 2.
- **References**: no bibliography; no ASWB URL (EPUB has no external links by design per QA). A short source list with names only (ASWB guidebook edition 08/2026, ASWB exam page) is safe.
- **Length for price**: about 17,700 words, 28 sections. Fair for a method book; thin against a 99-page illustrated PDF at $15.99 or a 113-page mock exam at $57 unless tools are added.

## 5. Market positioning

**Target customer.** A licensed-track social worker (MSW holder, supervised hours done) preparing for ASWB Clinical (LCSW). Two sub-groups: (a) retakers inside the 90-day wait, (b) first-timers who have booked or will book. The body of the book is written for (a).

**The problem.** They have the content outline and a blank study-plan template (both free from ASWB) and plenty of content products, but no decision about what to study on which day, and no way to know if they are behind. 2026 format changes (122 questions, three areas, ethics largest) make older materials mis-weighted.

**What makes them pay.** Fear of another $260 attempt and another wait; urgency (Aug 2026 format change); wanting a structure rather than more content; time scarcity (ten to twelve hours a week).

**What competitors offer (seen 2026-11-07).** Mostly question banks, mock exams, topic guides and flashcards (Pass With Ayo, Agents of Change, Test Treasure, MindX, Kaplan, Test Prep Books, Springer). Generic study planners (Sparky Mizzle free 5-page PDF, Zanini $9 Notion + ebook) are not exam-specific. I searched Gumroad, general web and Etsy listings and found no product that is a dated 90-day Clinical schedule with spaced review and pacing rehearsal. "None found" is not proof none exists.

**What they fail to provide.** A day-numbered sequence; rules for when you fall behind; spaced-review dates; a pacing rehearsal plan tied to the real 122-question structure; retake-specific handling of the score report; honest "no guarantee" language.

**What would make our book the better purchase.** (1) Say plainly who it is for on the cover and title; (2) add the printable tools; (3) add a PDF with the diagrams; (4) fix the accuracy items; (5) pair it, in the description, with ASWB's own practice test (the book already says it is the only representative material) so it reads as complement not substitute; (6) offer a clear refund wording; (7) collect first ratings.
