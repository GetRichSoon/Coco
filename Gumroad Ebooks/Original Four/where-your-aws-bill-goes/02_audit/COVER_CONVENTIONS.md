# Cover conventions for AWS cost and cloud guides on Gumroad (seen 2026-11-07)

Goal (owner update): commercially competitive, not luxury. Follow the niche's visual conventions, keep a recognisable Velvet Foundry publisher identity (a consistent small publisher mark and type pairing, not one colour for every book).

Covers viewed (saved in `competitor_covers\`): Cut Cloud Costs (`cut_cloud_costs.jpg`), Cloud Audit Best Practices (`cloud_audit_essentials.jpg`), Cut Cloud Costs 2-Pack (`cut_costs_2pack.jpg`), AWS Cost Optimization Checklist by Demarko (`demarko_checklist.jpg`), AWS Cost Savings and Kubernetes Optimization (`sidehustle_aws_cost_opt.jpg`), Anybody Can AWS (displayed via a re-render, file `anybody_can_aws.jpg` is 6400x9600), AWS Projects (`aws_projects.jpg`, tiny 300x150 thumbnail), AWS Serverless by Toledo (`serverless_aws_toledo.jpg`), Serverless Cookbook Plus (`serverless_cookbook.jpg`). ## What winning covers in this niche have in common
(Only observation; I have no sales data, so "winning" means the products that rank in search and have the most elaborate listing, not proven best-sellers.)

1. **Format.** Gumroad shows a 16:9 landscape cover (1280x720 or similar) first; every serious competitor designs for that frame, not for a portrait book jacket. The thumbnail is a crop of the same artwork.
2. **Colour.** Two families: (a) dark navy/black with one hot accent (Demarko: near-black blue, orange "AWS", cyan "47"); (b) loud flat yellow or orange blocks with black type (Cut Cloud Costs, the 2-pack, the sidehustle0 cover). Pastel/clip-art covers exist (Anybody Can AWS) but sit at the amateur end. Light "document" style covers are rare in this niche.
3. **Layout.** Left-aligned or stacked big title, a number-led benefit line, and a feature strip or checklist. The strongest one (Systems Architect) is a three-panel collage: feature list, title tile, real screenshot.
4. **Title size.** Title takes 40 to 60 percent of the width at 100+ px in the 1280 canvas; it remains readable when shrunk to a card.
5. **How the reader benefit is shown.** With numbers and nouns: "47 actionable checks", "850+ pages", "1,000+ Cost Savings Tips", "270+ checklists", plus the verb "Cut". Icons or tiles for service categories (Compute, Storage, Databases) make the scope scannable.
6. **Imagery.** Cloud shapes, laptop with a falling bar chart, check marks, scissors, a real console screenshot. Literal and instantly readable as "cloud cost".
7. **Badges.** "FULL VERSION", "UPDATED RECENTLY", "850+ pages", "Professional Series", "#1 ULTIMATE" (self-asserted; we must not copy superlatives). Dated editions ("2025") appear in titles.
8. **Publisher identity.** A small logo or series label top-left (DemarkoCloud, "Cloud Engineering Professional Series", "By the maker of ...").

## What looks amateur in this niche
- Clip-art illustrations unrelated to cost (game consoles, books and graduation caps, desks); pastel backgrounds.
- Typos on the cover ("WHITOUT WORRIES" on the Toledo cover).
- Title lost inside a decorative shape (the cloud outline with narrow condensed type).
- A cover that is only a title on a flat colour with no benefit (Serverless Cookbook).
- Tiny images (AWS Projects is 300x150).
- Using the AWS logo as the hero (done by three competitors: trademark exposure and looks derivative).
- Our current cover shares the "title only on dark gradient" problem, plus low-contrast grey words and an empty half.

## Rules for our book
- No AWS logo, smile arrow, service icons or colours copied from AWS (orange and navy are fine as a palette; do not combine them into an "AWS look").
- No "#1", "ultimate", "best", savings percentages or promised results on the cover.
- The words "Independent guide" or "Not affiliated with AWS" go on the poster in small type; keep as on the current poster.
- Benefit text on the cover must match the manuscript. The book contains no prices, no tips list count, and no checklist count other than per-chapter checklists (19 core chapters; 5 diagrams if the diagram edition ships). Use only numbers we can defend: "19 chapters", "5 pricing shapes", "1-hour monthly review", "5 diagrams" (only if adopted), "templates" (only if made).

## Thumbnail rules
At 600x600 and at the roughly 200 px card size: maximum three text elements (main title, one benefit line, publisher mark); title at least 90 px at 600 px; no text under 28 px at 600 px; contrast ratio 7:1 or higher for the title; no light-grey on dark; one focal image occupying one third of the square.

## Three distinct cover concepts (for the lead designer)

All three: 1280x720 poster (16:9) with a safe 40 px margin; the square thumbnail is a re-crop with the same elements stacked; title in a bold geometric or grotesque sans (e.g. Barlow, Montserrat or Inter, any licence-clear alternative) for category fit, subtitle in the same family; the small Velvet Foundry mark top-left in 22 px caps plus the "independent guide, not affiliated with AWS" line bottom-left in 18 px.

### Concept A: "The Bill, Opened" (receipt autopsy)
- Palette: warm off-white paper #f6f2ea background, ink black #101820 title, one hot accent orange-red #e8542f for the circled line, teal #1f8a8a for secondary.
- Layout: left 55 percent title and benefit; right 45 percent a stylised itemised bill (receipt tape style, fake labels only such as "Compute", "Storage", "Data transfer", "Requests", "Provisioned", no real prices) with one line circled in the accent colour and an annotation arrow reading "this is the one".
- Headline hierarchy: "WHERE YOUR AWS BILL GOES" (title, 3 lines, "AWS BILL" largest), subtitle "Understand it. Trace it. Reduce it.", benefit strip "5 pricing shapes - 19 chapters - 1-hour monthly review".
- Image idea: a long thin receipt running off the bottom edge, torn edge, one circled line; no logos. It reads instantly as "a bill" and is the visual metaphor for the title.
- Why a buyer recognises it: bill/receipt imagery matches the problem sentence ("someone forwarded me the invoice"); light background differs from the dark-navy crowd and stands out in a grid of dark covers; the circled line signals "find the cause".

### Concept B: "Five Meters" (the book's own framework as the cover)
- Palette: dark navy #0b1f33 background, cream #f4efe6 title, copper #d98e4a and teal #3aa6a0 as two accents (Velvet Foundry brand colours, but with a hot accent for pop and higher contrast than today).
- Layout: title top-left, large; across the bottom third five rounded tiles in a row, each a pictogram plus a one-word label: Time (clock), Storage (stacked disks), Requests (arrows), Transfer (two-way arrow across a dashed line), Capacity (padlock bar). Mirrors the Demarko tile strip but with the book's own taxonomy.
- Headline hierarchy: title "WHERE YOUR AWS BILL GOES", sub "The five shapes of cloud cost, and a monthly review that takes an hour", tiles as the benefit.
- Image idea: five tiles, each with a different accent tint, joined by a thin line so they read as one bill.
- Why a buyer recognises it: dark tech palette and icon tile strip are what the best-looking competitor uses, so it scans as "technical cost guide" instantly; the tiles tell the buyer what is inside without text.

### Concept C: "Cut the Spike" (the chart story)
- Palette: bright signal yellow #ffcf33 left block with black #0d0d0d title, right block charcoal #1c1f24 holding the graph, white graph line, one orange spike #ff6a2b.
- Layout: split 50/50. Left: big black title on yellow. Right: a daily cost line graph with a clear step up on one day, a thin circle around the step, the label "the 9th" (matches the book's worked month: the step on the 9th), and a small "Explain it in an hour" tag.
- Headline hierarchy: "WHERE YOUR AWS BILL GOES" (largest), "Find the step. Explain the bill." as a short benefit line, small subtitle below.
- Image idea: flat vector line chart with grid, no numbers on the axes (the book has none).
- Why a buyer recognises it: yellow/black is the convention of the best-known AWS cost guides on Gumroad, so it reads as "category", while the graph with a step is exactly what Cost Explorer shows when someone asks why the bill rose.

Recommendation for A/B testing: Concept B (closest to the dark competitor that looks most professional), Concept A (best differentiation in a grid). Concept C is the most category-conventional. There is no performance data, so pick by test on the live thumbnail (change one at a time, watch visits to purchases).
