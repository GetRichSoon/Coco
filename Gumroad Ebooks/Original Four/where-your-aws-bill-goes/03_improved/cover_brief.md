# Cover brief: "Where Your AWS Bill Goes"

The lead designer builds the covers; this file only briefs. See `..\02_audit\COVER_CONVENTIONS.md` for the competitor evidence and three full concepts (A "The Bill, Opened", B "Five Meters", C "Cut the Spike").

## What the book is
An independent plain-English guide to understanding and reducing an AWS bill. Five pricing shapes (time, storage, requests, data transfer, provisioned capacity), an order of operations (delete, resize, then commit), and a one-hour monthly review. About 15,000 words, EPUB (PDF if produced). No prices in the text.

## The reader
An engineer, tech lead or small-team owner who has been asked "why did the bill go up?" and cannot explain it. Practical, short of time, probably browsing Gumroad or Google with a query like "reduce aws bill" or "aws cost optimization". Not a finance specialist.

## Strongest single visual idea
A bill with one line circled: an itemised receipt (generic labels such as Compute, Storage, Data transfer, Requests, Provisioned; no real prices, no AWS styling) running off the bottom of the frame, one line circled in a hot accent with the annotation "this is the one". It is the title made visible, matches the opening scene of the book (a forwarded invoice nobody can explain), and the circled line promises the benefit (finding the cause). Fallback visual: a daily cost graph with a clear step up on one day (the book's worked month).

## Title hierarchy
1. Main title: WHERE YOUR AWS BILL GOES (largest word AWS BILL; fix the awkward "GOES" orphan by setting three lines as WHERE YOUR / AWS BILL / GOES in the same weight or two lines WHERE YOUR AWS BILL / GOES)
2. Benefit line (one short sentence): "Understand it. Trace it. Reduce it."
3. Subtitle (small, optional on thumbnail): "The five shapes of cloud cost, and a monthly review that takes an hour"
4. Publisher mark small: THE VELVET FOUNDRY. Author line optional on poster, omitted on thumbnail.
5. Disclaimer bottom-left on the poster only: "Independent guide. Not affiliated with or endorsed by Amazon Web Services."

## Palette suggestion
Choose per concept, but keep two Velvet Foundry anchors so the publisher stays recognisable: one navy (#0f2238 family) and one teal (#3aa6a0 family) used as small accents, plus a category-conventional hot accent (orange-red #e8542f or signal yellow #ffcf33). The dark navy and cream pair can be used for Concept B. Concept A uses a light paper background to stand out from the dark competitors; Concept C uses yellow and charcoal. Do not make every Velvet Foundry book dark and copper.

## Things to avoid
- AWS logos, the smile arrow, the AWS wordmark, service icons copied from AWS, "aws" in a cloud shape, Amazon colours combined into an AWS look. Generic icons (clock, disks, arrows, padlock) are fine.
- Any price, dollar sign with a figure, savings percentage, "#1", "ultimate", "best", "guaranteed", "cut your bill by".
- Clip-art unrelated to cost (consoles, graduation caps, stock desks), pastel backgrounds, text inside decorative shapes, typos.
- Light-grey text on dark (the current cover's "WHERE YOUR" and subtitle); text under 14 px on a 1280x720 poster.
- Claims the manuscript does not support: no "tools", "templates", "scripts" or "checklist count" on the cover unless they are shipped; "19 chapters", "5 pricing shapes", "1-hour monthly review" and "5 diagrams" (only if the diagram edition is adopted) are safe.

## Thumbnail legibility rules (600x600 and the roughly 200 px card)
- Maximum three text elements: title, one benefit line or numeric badge, publisher mark.
- Title at least 90 px at 600x600 (about 30 px on the card); contrast 7:1 or higher; no thin weights.
- Single focal image (circled receipt line, or the step on the graph) occupying about one third of the square; keep the circle and arrow at 6 px stroke or more.
- Test by shrinking to 200 px; the words WHERE YOUR AWS BILL GOES and the focal image must still be readable.
- Keep the same elements and colours as the poster so card and page match.
