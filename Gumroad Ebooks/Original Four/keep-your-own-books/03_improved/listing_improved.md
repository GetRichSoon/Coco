## Description (paste into Gumroad; plain text)

Keep Your Own Books is a plain-English bookkeeping guide for freelancers, sole traders and other one-person businesses.

You work for yourself, somebody told you to "keep your books", and nobody explained what that means. You have a business bank account, a folder of receipts and a worry that you are doing it wrong. This book teaches you one system, the double-entry method accountants use, so you can record your business properly, check it against your bank, and understand your own numbers.

WHAT YOU WILL BE ABLE TO DO
• Record any transaction your business produces, using both halves of the entry
• Prove your records against your bank statement with a monthly reconciliation
• Close a month in about an hour with a seven-step routine
• Read your own profit and loss and balance sheet
• Tell the difference between profit and cash, and know how much of your bank balance is really yours
• Keep records your accountant can use, so you pay for advice instead of tidying

WHAT IS INSIDE (20 chapters, about 15,000 words)
• The five kinds of account, and a test that sorts almost every transaction
• Double entry without the jargon, with five worked transactions
• A working chart of accounts for a one-person business (about thirty accounts)
• Recording money in and money out, including deposits, refunds, discounts and part payments
• Cash basis and accrual explained with the same December, both ways
• The bank reconciliation, with the three piles and what to do with each
• Drawings and owner's money, and why paying yourself is not an expense
• Untangling personal and business spending
• The one-hour month-end close
• Reading your profit and loss and your balance sheet
• A full month worked end to end, from opening position to closing statements
• What records to keep, and what your accountant actually wants
• When to stop doing it yourself
• Fifteen core chapters end with "When it goes wrong" and a checklist

WHAT MAKES IT DIFFERENT
This book contains no tax rates, thresholds, deadlines or filing rules. That is deliberate. Those rules depend on your country, change often, and are not what you need first. This book teaches how to record what happened, which is the same everywhere and does not expire. It names the three questions only a local professional can answer (your accounting basis, your capitalisation threshold, and how to split mixed personal and business costs) and tells you to ask them.

WHO IT IS FOR
Sole traders, sole proprietors, freelancers, independent contractors and single-owner businesses doing their own books. No accounting background needed.

WHO SHOULD NOT BUY IT
If you have employees and need payroll, run a limited company or corporation with filing duties, or want to be told your tax rates and deadlines, this is the wrong book. It says so in the "What this book does not do" chapter.

FORMAT
Reflowable EPUB (about 15,000 words), readable on a phone, tablet, e-reader or computer. ## FAQs (5)

**Do I need accounting software or experience?**
No. A spreadsheet is enough for a one-person business, and the book does not require you to buy software. If you already use bookkeeping software, the book explains the steps it performs behind the screen.

**Does it tell me what tax I owe or when to file?**
No. It contains no tax rates, thresholds, deadlines or filing instructions. Your tax authority or a qualified local professional gives those. The book helps you keep records good enough that they can help you quickly.

**Will it work in my country?**
The recording method is the same everywhere. The book flags the points where local rules apply (accounting basis, capitalisation threshold, mixed-use costs, record retention) and tells you to check them locally. The wording is British (sole trader, drawings, receivables); US readers will recognise the ideas under different names.

**I have a limited company or employees. Is this for me?**
Not on its own. The book is for sole traders and single-owner unincorporated businesses. Payroll, company filing and sales tax or VAT mechanics are out of scope, and the book says to take advice if they apply to you.
