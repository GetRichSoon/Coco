# Appendix B. Common mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Memorising sample answers | Memorise facts and structure, not sentences |
| Inventing stories | Use true ones; ask to move on if you have none |
| Talking too long | Aim for 60 to 120 seconds |
| Saying "we" only | Say what you did |
| Criticising past employers | State facts, focus on what you learned |
| Not researching the employer | Spend 45 minutes on the website and news |
| No questions at the end | Prepare at least four |
| Asking pay first | Wait until the offer or the employer raises it |
| Sending one resume everywhere | Tailor the top third |
| Not following up | Thank-you within 24 hours |
| Negotiating with false claims | Never claim offers you do not have |
| Ignoring tests of the video link | Test the day before |

# Appendix C. Glossary

**ATS (applicant tracking system):** Software employers use to receive and manage applications.

**Behavioural question:** A question about something you actually did.

**CAR:** Challenge, Action, Result.

**Case interview:** A scenario you analyse aloud.

**Cover letter:** A short letter explaining why you fit a role.

**Hiring manager:** The person who will manage the new hire.

**Offer:** A formal proposal of employment with terms.

**Panel interview:** An interview with several interviewers at once.

**Parsing:** Software extracting fields from a document.

**Situational question:** A hypothetical "what would you do".

**STAR:** Situation, Task, Action, Result.

**Structured interview:** The same questions and scoring for every candidate.

**Transferable skills:** Skills useful across roles or fields.

# Conclusion

You now have a method rather than a script. This week, do five things: write your master resume using the structure in Chapter 2; update LinkedIn using Chapter 4; fill in at least eight prompts from Appendix A; pick ten questions from Chapters 14 to 20 and answer them aloud from your real stories; and set up the tracker and week one of the 30 day planner in Chapter 13.

No book can promise an offer. What you can control is how clearly and truthfully you present real work. Do that consistently, learn from each interview, and adjust.
