# Chapter 18. Strengths, weaknesses and motivation (Q71 to Q80)

### Q71. What is your greatest strength?

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Whether your strength matches the job and is real.

**Answer framework:** One strength, proof, relevance.

**Example answer:** "Organisation. When I ran our department's open day I built a one-page schedule, assigned each volunteer a zone and held a ten-minute briefing. Nothing was double booked, and the head of department asked me to plan next year's. For this role, that same planning would help with your event calendar."

**Common mistakes:** Listing adjectives with no proof.

**Beginner adaptation:** Back it with a course or volunteer example.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Choose the strength most tied to the role's top need.

### Q72. What is your greatest weakness?

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Honesty and self-improvement.

**Answer framework:** A real, non-critical weakness, what you do about it, progress.

**Example answer:** "I used to avoid delegating because I wanted things done my way. It left me overloaded. I now write a short brief for each task, hand it over and check at agreed points. Last quarter I delegated three reports and still met all deadlines."

**Common mistakes:** "I work too hard" or a weakness that disqualifies you.

**Beginner adaptation:** Public speaking or time management, with steps you take.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** A management or technical gap with a proven fix.

### Q73. What skill are you currently developing?

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Learning drive.

**Answer framework:** The skill, why, how, progress.

**Example answer:** "Spreadsheet automation. I spent too long on repeated tasks, so I am taking a free course on macros and have already automated our weekly report, which saves about an hour."

**Common mistakes:** Naming a skill irrelevant to the job.

**Beginner adaptation:** Skills linked to the target field.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Adjacent skills that broaden your scope.

### Q74. What kind of work gives you energy?

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Fit with the role.

**Answer framework:** Type of work, example, link.

**Example answer:** "Explaining things to people who are lost. When I tutored first-year students in chemistry, the moment someone said 'oh, I see' made my day. That is why a training role appeals to me."

**Common mistakes:** Naming work the job does not have.

**Beginner adaptation:** Use class or volunteer experiences.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Show a pattern across roles.

### Q75. What does success mean to you?

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Values and ambition.

**Answer framework:** Personal and professional definition with a recent example.

**Example answer:** "Leaving things better than I found them. After my last project I left a handover document, and the next team started in a day instead of a week. That counts as success to me, along with meeting targets."

**Common mistakes:** Only money or title.

**Beginner adaptation:** Growth, learning and contribution.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Impact, people and results.

### Q76. How do you maintain work-life balance?

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Sustainability and boundaries.

**Answer framework:** Habits, flexibility, example.

**Example answer:** "I plan my week on Sunday, protect two evenings, and stay flexible at deadlines. In busy seasons I work longer, then take time back afterwards."

**Common mistakes:** Saying you never need balance, or implying low commitment.

**Beginner adaptation:** Balance of study, work and rest.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Boundaries with teams and clients.

### Q77. How do you stay motivated with repetitive tasks?

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Reliability.

**Answer framework:** Purpose, small improvements, routine.

**Example answer:** "I remind myself who the task serves. In my data entry job I set mini-targets and looked for errors to prevent. I found a pattern that saved checking time."

**Common mistakes:** Saying you hate repetition.

**Beginner adaptation:** Entry-level repetitive tasks.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Automating or delegating.

### Q78. What are you most passionate about?

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Genuine interests and energy.

**Answer framework:** Passion, evidence, link to work.

**Example answer:** "Accessible design. I volunteered to test a charity's website with a screen reader and found six issues. They fixed them, and I enjoyed it so much I started studying accessibility standards."

**Common mistakes:** Passion with no evidence.

**Beginner adaptation:** Courses and volunteering.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Long-term pursuit with impact.

### Q79. What do you value in a workplace culture?

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Cultural fit.

**Answer framework:** Two values, example, how you contribute.

**Example answer:** "Open communication and learning from mistakes. At my last job we held a short review after each project, and I found it made us better. I would like to continue that."

**Common mistakes:** Naming values the employer clearly lacks.

**Beginner adaptation:** Student teams and part-time workplaces.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Compare with cultures you've worked in.

### Q80. What is a strength of yours that is not obvious on your resume?

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Hidden value.

**Answer framework:** Strength, evidence, relevance.

**Example answer:** "Calm in conflict. It's not a job title, but at the restaurant I often settled disputes between guests and staff. I listen, summarise, and offer options. It helps with client calls."

**Common mistakes:** Repeating a resume item.

**Beginner adaptation:** Life experience, caring roles.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Mentoring, cross-cultural skills, language.

# Chapter 19. Career and salary questions (Q81 to Q90)

### Q81. What salary are you expecting?

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Whether your expectations fit their budget.

**Answer framework:** Researched range, flexibility, ask theirs.

**Example answer:** "From my research on similar roles in this city, I'm looking at a range of 38,000 to 44,000, depending on the full package. I would like to understand the scope of the role better. What range have you budgeted?"

**Common mistakes:** Naming a number without research.

**Beginner adaptation:** Use public pay data for entry roles.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Anchor on recent comparable roles and your scope.

### Q82. What is your current salary?

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Your baseline or their anchor.

**Answer framework:** Rules differ by location. Redirect to the role's range, or answer truthfully if you choose.

**Example answer:** "I would prefer to focus on the value of this role. Based on market research, my target is about 52,000. If it helps, I can share my total package for context."

**Common mistakes:** Lying.

**Beginner adaptation:** Say you are seeking your first salary and ask the range.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Frame your total package and scope.

### Q83. When can you start?

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Availability.

**Answer framework:** Notice period or date, flexibility.

**Example answer:** "My contract requires one month's notice, so I could begin on the first of next month. I'm happy to discuss a handover plan."

**Common mistakes:** Promising a date you cannot meet.

**Beginner adaptation:** State your graduation or exam end date.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** State notice and handover.

### Q84. Do you have other offers or interviews?

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Competition and interest.

**Answer framework:** Be honest, show preference.

**Example answer:** "I'm in the process with two other companies, but this role is my first choice because of the team. I'd have a decision by the end of the month."

**Common mistakes:** Inventing offers.

**Beginner adaptation:** Say you're interviewing and prefer this role.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Share timelines carefully.

### Q85. Are you willing to relocate or travel?

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Flexibility.

**Answer framework:** Honest limits and conditions.

**Example answer:** "I can travel up to a quarter of the time. Relocation would depend on the package and timing, as my partner works locally. I am happy to discuss."

**Common mistakes:** Saying yes then refusing later.

**Beginner adaptation:** Show flexibility if true.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Discuss family and relocation support.

### Q86. How flexible are you on salary?

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Negotiation stance.

**Answer framework:** Priorities, total package.

**Example answer:** "There is some flexibility if the role offers growth and training. I'd look at the whole package, including development budget and review dates."

**Common mistakes:** Saying you will accept anything.

**Beginner adaptation:** Emphasise learning value, within fair pay.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Anchor on market and total rewards.

### Q87. Why is there a gap in your employment history?

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Explanation, not judgement.

**Answer framework:** Short fact, what you did, readiness.

**Example answer:** "I took eight months to care for a family member. During that time I completed a bookkeeping certificate. I'm now fully available and ready to return."

**Common mistakes:** Over-apologising or lying.

**Beginner adaptation:** Gap between school and work: mention study, projects.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Name the reason briefly, show currency.

### Q88. Why have you changed jobs frequently?

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Stability and pattern.

**Answer framework:** Reasons, growth, commitment now.

**Example answer:** "Two roles were fixed-term contracts and one company closed. Each gave me new skills, such as inventory systems. I'm looking for a longer-term place."

**Common mistakes:** Blaming employers.

**Beginner adaptation:** Internships and seasonal work explain short stints.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Show the career thread.

### Q89. Why are you changing careers?

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Commitment and transferable skills.

**Answer framework:** Reason, evidence of preparation, transferable skills.

**Example answer:** "After eight years teaching, I found I most enjoyed designing materials. I completed a certificate in instructional design and built three modules for a charity. My classroom experience helps me understand learners."

**Common mistakes:** Disparaging your old field.

**Beginner adaptation:** Explain interest shift from your degree.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Highlight transferable results.

### Q90. Would you accept a lower position or pay?

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Motivation and realism.

**Answer framework:** Honest conditions and long-term interest.

**Example answer:** "I'm open to it if there is a clear route to grow, as I'm changing fields. I'd want to discuss expectations and a review point."

**Common mistakes:** Agreeing and resenting later.

**Beginner adaptation:** Show willingness to start at the bottom.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Be clear on non-negotiables.

# Chapter 20. Challenging and unexpected questions (Q91 to Q100)

### Q91. If you were a kitchen tool, which would you be?

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Creativity and composure.

**Answer framework:** Pick quickly, give a work-related reason.

**Example answer:** "A sharp knife: precise and useful when carefully used. I like to cut a task into clear pieces. I also know a knife needs care, so I rest and sharpen my skills."

**Common mistakes:** Refusing or joking only.

**Beginner adaptation:** Keep it light and link to your study style.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Link to a leadership trait.

### Q92. Sell me this pen.

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Sales approach and listening.

**Answer framework:** Ask needs first, match feature to need, close.

**Example answer:** "Before I describe it, can I ask what you usually write with, and what you dislike about it? If you sign contracts often, this pen's smooth ink would help. Would you like to try it?"

**Common mistakes:** Listing features without questions.

**Beginner adaptation:** Practise asking questions.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Show consultative selling.

### Q93. Tell me something that is not on your resume.

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Personality and depth.

**Answer framework:** A real fact with relevance.

**Example answer:** "I taught myself basic Spanish in a year using audio lessons and a conversation partner. It shows I stick with long goals."

**Common mistakes:** Over-sharing private details.

**Beginner adaptation:** Interests that show discipline.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Unusual projects.

### Q94. What would you do if you won the lottery?

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Values and commitment.

**Answer framework:** Honest, relevant, show you enjoy work.

**Example answer:** "I'd help my family and travel, but I'd likely still work in education because I enjoy it. I might choose projects with more freedom."

**Common mistakes:** Saying you'd quit tomorrow.

**Beginner adaptation:** Show purpose.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Show sustainable commitment.

### Q95. What is your biggest regret?

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Reflection.

**Answer framework:** Real regret, lesson, action.

**Example answer:** "I did not ask for a mentor early. I struggled alone for a year. Now I seek mentors and have two."

**Common mistakes:** Saying none.

**Beginner adaptation:** A study choice you adjusted.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** A career lesson.

### Q96. You don't meet one of our requirements. How will you cover that gap?

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Honesty and learning plan.

**Answer framework:** Acknowledge, show related experience, plan.

**Example answer:** "That's right, I haven't used your CRM, though I've used two similar tools. I'd complete your training in the first week and ask a colleague to review my first entries."

**Common mistakes:** Denying the gap.

**Beginner adaptation:** Point to fast learning.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Show prior ramp-up speed.

### Q97. You see a colleague cutting corners. What do you do?

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Integrity and judgement.

**Answer framework:** Check facts, speak to them, escalate if serious.

**Example answer:** "I would first check I understood. If it's a safety or compliance issue, I'd speak to them privately, and if it continued, report it to my supervisor."

**Common mistakes:** Ignoring it.

**Beginner adaptation:** Show willingness to ask.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Cite policy and reporting channels.

### Q98. Have you ever been fired or asked to leave?

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Honesty and growth.

**Answer framework:** Truth, ownership, lesson.

**Example answer:** "Yes, from a sales job in 2019. I missed targets and had not asked for help. I took a role in customer service, learned the product, and have met my targets since."

**Common mistakes:** Lying or blaming.

**Beginner adaptation:** If never, say so; if from a part-time job, be brief.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Frame calmly with facts.

### Q99. Is there anything we have not asked that you would like us to know?

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Your closing priorities.

**Answer framework:** Summarise fit, add one missed point.

**Example answer:** "We haven't discussed my customer survey work. It gave me experience in analysing feedback, which fits your role. Overall, I think my background matches your needs."

**Common mistakes:** "No" or a rambling closing.

**Beginner adaptation:** One missed project.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Summarise best fit.

### Q100. What concerns might we have about hiring you?

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Self-awareness and confidence.

**Answer framework:** Name the likely concern, give evidence against it.

**Example answer:** "You may worry that I'm new to the industry. I've shadowed an analyst for two weeks and built a sample report. I learn quickly and ask good questions."

**Common mistakes:** Getting defensive.

**Beginner adaptation:** Address lack of experience.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Address overqualification or salary.
