# Chapter 16. Teamwork and leadership questions (Q41 to Q55)

### Q41. How do you work with a difficult team member?

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Your ability to cooperate when it is hard.

**Answer framework:** Understand the cause, talk directly, focus on the task, escalate only if needed.

**Example answer:** "A teammate on a data project rarely replied to messages. I asked him privately what was blocking him. He was overloaded with another course. We agreed shorter tasks and fixed check-ins, and the report was finished on time."

**Common mistakes:** Complaining about the person.

**Beginner adaptation:** Use a group assignment.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Use a cross-team stakeholder and show escalation judgement.

### Q42. What role do you usually take in a team?

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Self-awareness and flexibility.

**Answer framework:** Usual role, evidence, how you flex.

**Example answer:** "I tend to be the organiser: I keep the task list and check deadlines. On a design project I noticed I was talking too much, so I stepped back and let a quieter member lead the presentation. It went better."

**Common mistakes:** Saying you are always the leader or always the follower.

**Beginner adaptation:** Sport, class or club teams.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Describe different roles in different team stages.

### Q43. Tell me about a conflict with a coworker.

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Maturity in disagreement.

**Answer framework:** Facts, conversation, shared goal, resolution.

**Example answer:** "A colleague and I disagreed over who should update the weekly report. I asked to talk, we listed tasks on a whiteboard, and agreed to alternate weeks. The report has been on time since."

**Common mistakes:** Making yourself the hero and the other person the villain.

**Beginner adaptation:** Flatmates, club or project conflicts that show process.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Interdepartmental conflict handled with data and a clear compromise.

### Q44. Tell me about a time you led a team or project.

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Leadership behaviour.

**Answer framework:** Goal, how you organised people, obstacles, result.

**Example answer:** "I led a team of five to organise a charity quiz night. I assigned tasks by strength, held a short weekly check-in, and handled the venue negotiation myself. We raised money for the local hospice and finished under budget."

**Common mistakes:** Using "we" so much your role is unclear.

**Beginner adaptation:** Society, project or sports captaincy.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Scale, budget, people, and your decisions.

### Q45. How do you motivate others?

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Leadership style.

**Answer framework:** Understand what each person values, give clear goals, recognise effort.

**Example answer:** "I ask what each person wants from the work. One volunteer wanted to learn social media, so I gave her the campaign. Another liked structure, so I gave her the schedule. Both stayed until the end."

**Common mistakes:** Saying "I lead by example" and stopping.

**Beginner adaptation:** Group leadership, tutoring.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Include feedback cycles, targets and development.

### Q46. How do you give feedback to a peer?

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Tact and directness.

**Answer framework:** Private, specific, behaviour not personality, ask for their view, agree next step.

**Example answer:** "A colleague's slides were hard to read. I asked if he wanted feedback, then said the font was small for a large room and suggested a layout. He changed it and thanked me."

**Common mistakes:** Avoiding feedback or being harsh.

**Beginner adaptation:** Peer review in coursework.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Include follow-up and a documented outcome.

### Q47. How do you work in a remote or distributed team?

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Communication discipline.

**Answer framework:** Clear written updates, agreed hours, tools, check-ins.

**Example answer:** "On a project with members in three time zones we agreed core overlap hours, posted daily written updates, and recorded decisions in a shared document. It prevented repeated questions and kept us on schedule."

**Common mistakes:** Saying remote is the same as in-person.

**Beginner adaptation:** Online coursework groups.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Team norms and handover practices.

### Q48. What do you do if a teammate causes you to miss a deadline?

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Accountability and problem-solving.

**Answer framework:** Raise early, fix what you can, communicate to the stakeholder, learn.

**Example answer:** "I would tell my teammate straight away what I need and by when. If it still slipped, I would inform our lead with a revised date and a plan, without blaming. Afterwards I would agree earlier check-ins."

**Common mistakes:** Blaming without solutions.

**Beginner adaptation:** Group project example.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Cross-team dependency management.

### Q49. How would you handle a team member who isn't pulling their weight?

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Fairness and courage.

**Answer framework:** Private conversation, find cause, clarify expectations, escalate if needed.

**Example answer:** "I would ask privately whether something was getting in the way, explain what I needed, and offer help. If it continued, I would raise it with the supervisor and share facts, not opinions."

**Common mistakes:** Going straight to the manager or ignoring it.

**Beginner adaptation:** Group coursework.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Include performance management and support.

### Q50. How do you delegate work?

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Trust and clarity.

**Answer framework:** Match task to person, define outcome, give resources, check in.

**Example answer:** "I explain the goal and the deadline, not every step. I ask what support they need and book a short check-in halfway. When I delegated the supplier calls to a junior colleague, he found a better price."

**Common mistakes:** Micromanaging, or delegating without support.

**Beginner adaptation:** Distributing tasks in a society.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Show trade-offs and developing people.

### Q51. How do you build relationships with stakeholders?

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Influence and communication.

**Answer framework:** Identify, understand needs, regular updates, reliability.

**Example answer:** "I list who is affected by the work and ask each what they need to know and how often. For the building project I sent a Friday summary to the residents, which reduced complaints."

**Common mistakes:** Only contacting stakeholders when there is a problem.

**Beginner adaptation:** Clubs, community or group clients.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Senior stakeholder mapping.

### Q52. How do you manage a team member who is underperforming?

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Management judgement.

**Answer framework:** Clear facts, listen, set expectations, support, review.

**Example answer:** "I would meet privately, show specific examples of the gap, and ask for their view. We would agree two or three targets and weekly reviews. If there is no improvement, I would follow the company process with HR."

**Common mistakes:** Avoiding the conversation, or being punitive.

**Beginner adaptation:** Describe how you'd approach it, with a peer-mentoring example.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Give a real case, process followed and the result.

### Q53. What is your leadership style?

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Self-awareness and fit.

**Answer framework:** Core approach, example, adaptation.

**Example answer:** "I lead by setting clear goals and then giving people room. With newer staff I check in more often, and with experienced ones I step back. When my team was short-staffed, I took the least popular shift myself."

**Common mistakes:** Buzzwords such as "servant leader" without evidence.

**Beginner adaptation:** Describe how you lead group projects.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Link style to team outcomes.

### Q54. How do you work with people very different from you?

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Respect and adaptability.

**Answer framework:** Curiosity, shared goals, communication, example.

**Example answer:** "On a summer job with staff of all ages, I asked each person how they preferred to receive instructions. One older colleague wanted written notes, a younger one a quick chat. Adjusting made the team smoother."

**Common mistakes:** Saying you treat everyone the same.

**Beginner adaptation:** Student groups, societies, exchange programmes.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** International or cross-functional teams.

### Q55. Tell me about a time you helped a struggling colleague.

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Empathy and teamwork.

**Answer framework:** Notice, offer help, support without taking over, outcome.

**Example answer:** "A new colleague was falling behind with order entry. I offered to sit with her for twenty minutes each morning and showed my shortcut. In two weeks she was at normal speed."

**Common mistakes:** Doing their work for them.

**Beginner adaptation:** Study group or volunteering.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Mentoring and development outcomes.

# Chapter 17. Problem-solving questions (Q56 to Q70)

### Q56. Describe a complex problem you solved.

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Analytical thinking.

**Answer framework:** Define, break down, test options, decide, check.

**Example answer:** "Our delivery vans were often late. I mapped routes and times for two weeks and found most delays came from one loading bay queue. I proposed staggered loading slots. Late deliveries fell the next month."

**Common mistakes:** Skipping how you analysed.

**Beginner adaptation:** Coursework or personal project.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Complex multi-factor problems with data.

### Q57. How do you approach analysing data?

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Method and rigour.

**Answer framework:** Question, clean data, explore, test, communicate.

**Example answer:** "I start with the question, check the data for gaps, look at simple summaries, then test one idea at a time. I always check my numbers against a known total before presenting."

**Common mistakes:** Naming tools only.

**Beginner adaptation:** Describe a course project.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Include business decisions and limits.

### Q58. How do you prioritise when everything is urgent?

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Judgement.

**Answer framework:** Clarify deadlines and impact, ask the manager, communicate trade-offs.

**Example answer:** "I list tasks by deadline and consequence, then ask my manager to confirm the order if two conflict. If a task must wait, I tell the person affected and give a new time."

**Common mistakes:** Saying you do everything at once.

**Beginner adaptation:** Exams and assignments.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Stakeholder trade-offs.

### Q59. Tell me about a time you noticed a problem others missed.

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Attention to detail and speaking up.

**Answer framework:** What you saw, how you checked, who you told, result.

**Example answer:** "While counting stock I saw that one product's quantity did not match deliveries. I checked invoices and found a supplier had shipped short for two months. We received a credit."

**Common mistakes:** Claiming credit for team discoveries.

**Beginner adaptation:** Lab or coursework example.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Financial or operational risk.

### Q60. Describe a creative solution you came up with.

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Creativity within constraints.

**Answer framework:** Problem, options, chosen idea, result.

**Example answer:** "Our library had few visitors on Fridays. I suggested a quiet study hour with free tea. A few weeks later, Friday visits had roughly doubled by the staff's count."

**Common mistakes:** Vague ideas without results.

**Beginner adaptation:** Club or study ideas.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Business improvements with cost.

### Q61. What do you do when you don't know the answer?

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Honesty and resourcefulness.

**Answer framework:** Say so, state what you know, find out, check.

**Example answer:** "I say I am not sure, share what I do know, and tell them how I will find the answer. At the helpdesk I would check the knowledge base, ask a senior colleague and then call the customer back."

**Common mistakes:** Bluffing.

**Beginner adaptation:** Use a learning moment from study.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Include whom you consult and how you document.

### Q62. How would you estimate how many coffee shops a city has?

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Structured reasoning, not the exact number.

**Answer framework:** State assumptions, break into parts, compute, sanity check.

**Example answer:** "I would assume the city has 500,000 people, and one shop for about 2,500 people near commercial areas. 500,000 divided by 2,500 is 200. Then I would adjust: city centres have more, so I might say between 200 and 300, and check against a business directory."

**Common mistakes:** Silence or giving a number without steps.

**Beginner adaptation:** Practise with simple arithmetic and say assumptions.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Use data sources you would check.

### Q63. What would you do in your first 90 days?

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Planning and realism.

**Answer framework:** Learn, build relationships, deliver a small win.

**Example answer:** "First month: learn the systems, meet the team and customers. Second: take on part of the workload and propose one improvement. Third: deliver a small project and review with my manager."

**Common mistakes:** Promising to change everything.

**Beginner adaptation:** Learning, support, small tasks.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Listen first, then change with data.

### Q64. How do you handle unclear instructions?

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Communication and initiative.

**Answer framework:** Clarify, restate, confirm in writing.

**Example answer:** "I ask what the goal is, who will use the output and when it's due. Then I send a short note: 'My understanding is...' If something changes, I update it."

**Common mistakes:** Guessing silently.

**Beginner adaptation:** Assignments with vague briefs.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Senior stakeholder requests.

### Q65. What process would you improve in this role?

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Insight and humility.

**Answer framework:** Observe first, hypothesise, ask how it works now.

**Example answer:** "I do not know your process yet, but from the posting I would ask how orders are tracked. If it is manual, I would test a shared spreadsheet before suggesting anything bigger."

**Common mistakes:** Criticising the employer without knowing.

**Beginner adaptation:** Offer a general approach, ask questions.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Use a comparable improvement you made.

### Q66. A project is falling behind schedule. What do you do?

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Project judgement.

**Answer framework:** Diagnose, options, communicate, re-plan.

**Example answer:** "I would find where the delay is, then list options: reduce scope, add help, or move the date. I would tell the sponsor early with a recommended option, and update the plan."

**Common mistakes:** Hiding delays.

**Beginner adaptation:** Group project example.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Cost and risk trade-offs.

### Q67. A customer complains about a product. What do you do?

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Service and process.

**Answer framework:** Listen, apologise, solve, record, follow up.

**Example answer:** "I would listen, apologise for the experience, check the details, offer a fix or refund within policy, and record the issue so the team can spot patterns. I would follow up in a week."

**Common mistakes:** Arguing or promising outside policy.

**Beginner adaptation:** Retail or hospitality experience.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Escalation and root cause.

### Q68. Two deadlines clash. What do you do?

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Prioritisation.

**Answer framework:** Impact, ask, negotiate, communicate.

**Example answer:** "I would find which has the larger impact, then talk to both requesters. Often one can move by a day. I would agree a plan, deliver the first, and keep the second person updated."

**Common mistakes:** Silently dropping one.

**Beginner adaptation:** Coursework clash.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Between managers.

### Q69. How do you make decisions?

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Decision-making process.

**Answer framework:** Gather facts, options, risks, decide, review.

**Example answer:** "I define the decision and the deadline, collect key facts, list options with pros and cons, and ask someone to challenge my choice. Then I decide and review the outcome."

**Common mistakes:** Always deferring to others.

**Beginner adaptation:** Choosing methods in a project.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** High-stakes example.

### Q70. How do you make sure your work is accurate?

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Quality control.

**Answer framework:** Checks, tools, second pair of eyes, learn from errors.

**Example answer:** "I use a checklist, compare totals with the source, and read the work after a break. For important reports I ask a colleague to review. I log errors to find patterns."

**Common mistakes:** Claiming you never make errors.

**Beginner adaptation:** Proofreading and checks in coursework.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Team review systems.
