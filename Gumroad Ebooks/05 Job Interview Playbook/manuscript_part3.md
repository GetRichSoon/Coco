# Chapter 14. Introductory and personal questions (Q1 to Q20)

These questions open most interviews. Every example answer below is a fictional illustration: use it to see the shape, then build your own from true experience. Do not memorise or copy it.

### Q1. Tell me about yourself.

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Whether you can summarise your relevant background clearly and start the conversation well.

**Answer framework:** Past, present, future: one relevant past step, current focus, why this role. Under 90 seconds.

**Example answer:** "I studied environmental science and spent last summer at a river-monitoring charity, where I entered water-sample data and spotted a recording error in one site's readings. Now I am finishing my dissertation on urban run-off. I am applying here because I want to turn that analytical work into practical policy, and your team's local projects fit that."

**Common mistakes:** Reciting your life story, repeating the resume, or listing hobbies only.

**Beginner adaptation:** Lead with study, projects and one volunteer or part-time experience tied to the job.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Start with your current scope, one major result, and why the next step is this role.

### Q2. Walk me through your resume.

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Whether your career story is coherent and whether you can explain choices.

**Answer framework:** Chronological headlines, one sentence per role: what you did, what you learned, why you moved.

**Example answer:** "I started as a pharmacy assistant, learning stock control and customer care. I moved to a clinic receptionist role to work with patients directly, then into appointment coordination, where I redesigned the weekly schedule. Each move added responsibility. This role combines scheduling and patient contact, which are the parts I enjoy most."

**Common mistakes:** Reading dates aloud, spending too long on the oldest roles.

**Beginner adaptation:** Walk through education first, then projects and jobs, linking each to the role.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Compress early roles to one sentence; spend most time on the last two.

### Q3. Why did you apply for this role?

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Whether your interest is specific and genuine.

**Answer framework:** Two reasons from the posting, one skill you bring, one honest personal reason.

**Example answer:** "Your posting stresses forecasting for small retailers, which I did in my statistics project using a local shop's sales. I like work where numbers lead to a decision a real owner can act on. The role also lets me use Python, which I want to deepen. Those three things made this the first application I wrote."

**Common mistakes:** Generic praise, mentioning only salary or location.

**Beginner adaptation:** Link a course, project or placement directly to a posting requirement.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Name the problem the role solves and your past evidence of solving similar ones.

### Q4. Why do you want to work for our company?

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Whether you researched them and are likely to stay.

**Answer framework:** One fact you learned, why it matters to you, how you would contribute.

**Example answer:** "I read your annual report and noticed you opened two community kitchens last year. I volunteered at a food bank for two years and saw how much a stable local kitchen matters. I would like to work for an organisation that measures impact by meals served, and my events experience could help with fundraising."

**Common mistakes:** Quoting the mission statement, flattering without evidence.

**Beginner adaptation:** Use a product you have used, an event you attended or a talk by their staff.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Connect a company strategy or challenge to a result you have delivered.

### Q5. What do you know about our organisation?

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Your preparation and curiosity.

**Answer framework:** Three facts (what they do, who they serve, recent development) and one question.

**Example answer:** "You design modular school furniture, sell mainly to local authorities, and recently launched a recycled-plastic range. I read that tender cycles are long, so I would like to ask how the team keeps relationships warm between tenders. I am particularly interested in the recycled line because of my product design coursework."

**Common mistakes:** Reciting the About page, or admitting you did not look.

**Beginner adaptation:** Use the website, a news item and one customer review you read.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Include a competitor comparison and a risk or opportunity you see.

### Q6. What are your career goals?

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Whether your direction fits what the role can offer.

**Answer framework:** Short-term skill goal, medium-term responsibility goal, and how this role is a step.

**Example answer:** "In the next two years I want to be solid at clinical documentation and ward routines. After that I would like to take on mentoring student nurses, because I enjoyed supervising a first-year on placement. A hospital with a structured preceptor programme like yours is a good fit for both."

**Common mistakes:** "I want your job", or having no goals.

**Beginner adaptation:** Name skills you want to master and the field you are building toward.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Name the scope you want next and show how this employer can provide it.

### Q7. Where do you see yourself in five years?

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Ambition, realism, and likelihood of staying.

**Answer framework:** Skills, scope, contribution. Avoid exact titles you cannot control.

**Example answer:** "I hope to have become the person the team trusts for complex customer cases, and perhaps to be training new joiners. I would like to understand the product deeply, not just the support scripts. If the company grows, I would be glad to take more responsibility."

**Common mistakes:** Saying you will start a business, or "I don't know" without a thought.

**Beginner adaptation:** Show willingness to learn and to take on more with time.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Show where you can create value and how your past leads to a larger remit.

### Q8. What motivates you?

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** What drives your effort and whether the role supplies it.

**Answer framework:** One true motivator, one example, link to the job.

**Example answer:** "Fixing things that waste people's time. At a call centre I noticed callers repeating account details three times, so I proposed a note field and a short checklist. Calls shortened by about a minute according to my supervisor's sample. Seeing a daily annoyance disappear motivates me more than targets."

**Common mistakes:** Saying only money, or a vague "challenge".

**Beginner adaptation:** Draw on study, volunteering or sport where you saw this motivation.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Cite a pattern across roles and one large example.

### Q9. Why did you choose your degree or field of study?

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Your decision-making and interest.

**Answer framework:** Origin of interest, what you learned, how it connects to the job.

**Example answer:** "I took a statistics elective expecting it to be dull and found I loved asking whether a result was real. That led me to choose a data-focused economics degree. The course taught me to question numbers, which is exactly what an audit role needs."

**Common mistakes:** Blaming parents or luck, or saying you regret it.

**Beginner adaptation:** Link a favourite module to the role's tasks.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Explain briefly, then show how your career has built on or moved from it.

### Q10. What do you do outside work or study?

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Personality, balance and transferable traits.

**Answer framework:** One or two true activities, a trait they show.

**Example answer:** "I coach an under-12 football team on Saturdays. It taught me to explain things simply to people who do not want to listen. I also cycle to work, which keeps me alert. Both help me stay patient in busy weeks."

**Common mistakes:** Listing many hobbies, or hiding that you have none.

**Beginner adaptation:** Societies, sport, creative projects, caring responsibilities.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Mention anything that shows long-term commitment, such as mentoring or board roles.

### Q11. How would your colleagues describe you?

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Self-awareness and reputation.

**Answer framework:** Two traits, each backed by something a colleague actually said or did.

**Example answer:** "Reliable and calm. My supervisor at the warehouse once said she gave me the late-shift handovers because nothing got dropped. A teammate also told me I explain procedures clearly. I try to deserve both."

**Common mistakes:** Claiming praise that you cannot support.

**Beginner adaptation:** Quote feedback from tutors, teammates or managers.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Use performance review language and one concrete comment.

### Q12. What are you looking for in your next role?

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Whether the role can satisfy you.

**Answer framework:** Three needs that match the job: skills, type of work, environment.

**Example answer:** "I want a role where I do hands-on design work and get feedback from senior designers. I also want to see my work used by real customers. Your studio ships small client projects every month, which suits both."

**Common mistakes:** Listing needs that the job obviously cannot meet.

**Beginner adaptation:** Emphasise learning, mentorship and breadth.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Emphasise scope, autonomy and impact.

### Q13. Why are you leaving your current job?

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Your attitude and reliability.

**Answer framework:** Positive pull toward this role; one neutral fact; no blame.

**Example answer:** "I have enjoyed four years in the bank, but the branch has no route into the commercial team, which is where I want to work. Your role offers client work with small firms. I am leaving on good terms and will work my full notice."

**Common mistakes:** Blaming a manager, or sharing salary frustration first.

**Beginner adaptation:** For a first move, say you want more responsibility or a better skills match.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Frame the change as a growth step or restructure, factually.

### Q14. What is your proudest achievement?

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** What you value and what you can do.

**Answer framework:** STAR, with a result and your personal part.

**Example answer:** "Finishing my first marathon after a knee injury. I built a twelve-week plan with a physiotherapist, kept a log, and adjusted when pain returned. I finished in five hours, slowly but without injury. It taught me patience and structured planning, which I use in project work."

**Common mistakes:** Boasting about team results as yours alone.

**Beginner adaptation:** A project, competition or personal goal is fine.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Pick one with measurable business impact and your specific role.

### Q15. How do you keep up to date in your field?

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Curiosity and self-development.

**Answer framework:** Two concrete habits and a recent example of something learned.

**Example answer:** "I follow two industry newsletters and join a monthly local meetup. Last month I learned about a new reporting standard there and checked how it affects our template. I keep short notes and share a summary with my team."

**Common mistakes:** Saying "I read the news" without specifics.

**Beginner adaptation:** Courses, professional student bodies, free resources.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Speaking, teaching, certifications and how you apply learning.

### Q16. What does a good workday look like for you?

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Working style and fit.

**Answer framework:** Describe rhythm, collaboration, outcomes.

**Example answer:** "A focused morning on the hardest task, a short check-in with teammates, and an afternoon of meetings and replies. I finish having moved one important thing forward and left clear notes for tomorrow."

**Common mistakes:** Describing a day with no work or no people.

**Beginner adaptation:** Base it on study or placement days.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Include how you balance delegation and hands-on work.

### Q17. Describe your ideal manager.

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Your needs and whether you can work with their style.

**Answer framework:** Two qualities, an example of a good past manager, flexibility.

**Example answer:** "Someone who is clear about priorities and gives regular feedback. My team lead in the café did a five-minute review every Friday, and I improved quickly. I adapt to different styles, but clear expectations help me most."

**Common mistakes:** Criticising past managers.

**Beginner adaptation:** Mention tutors, coaches or supervisors you learned from.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Mention how you work with managers who are hands-off or detailed.

### Q18. Why should we hire you?

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Your fit against their needs.

**Answer framework:** Their need, your matching evidence, your difference.

**Example answer:** "You need someone who can run supplier orders from day one. I have run weekly orders for a restaurant for two years without stockouts on core items, and I know the software you use. I also learn quickly, as I did when I took over the allergen records."

**Common mistakes:** Comparing yourself to other candidates.

**Beginner adaptation:** Combine skills, learning speed and motivation, with proof.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Lead with a result directly relevant to the role's biggest need.

### Q19. How did you hear about this role?

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Your search habits and genuine interest.

**Answer framework:** The honest source and what drew you.

**Example answer:** "A former colleague, Maria, who works in your logistics team, told me the planner role had opened. I read the description that night and saw it matches the scheduling work I do now."

**Common mistakes:** Lying about a referral, or saying you applied to everything.

**Beginner adaptation:** Career fair, university board, society event.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Recruiter, network, or following the company.

### Q20. Do you have any questions for us?

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Engagement and judgement.

**Answer framework:** Three prepared questions on success, team and next steps.

**Example answer:** "Yes. What would you expect me to have achieved by the end of six months? How does the team handle disagreements about priorities? And what are the next steps in the process?"

**Common mistakes:** Saying "No", or asking only about pay and holiday.

**Beginner adaptation:** Ask about training, mentoring and typical first projects.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Ask about strategy, team gaps and measures of success.

# Chapter 15. Behavioural questions (Q21 to Q40)

Each answer here should use a real STAR story from your own bank (Chapter 7).

### Q21. Tell me about a time you failed.

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Honesty, accountability and learning.

**Answer framework:** STAR plus Learning; own the cause; show what changed.

**Example answer:** "I missed a grant deadline because I assumed our partner would send the budget. I never confirmed. We submitted a day late and lost the round. I apologised to my director, wrote a checklist with a two-day confirmation step, and have used it on every application since. We have not missed a deadline in the three submissions after."

**Common mistakes:** Choosing a fake failure, or blaming others.

**Beginner adaptation:** A study, sport or part-time work failure is fine.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Pick a real professional miss with consequences and a lasting process change.

### Q22. Tell me about a time you met a tight deadline.

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Planning, prioritisation, and composure.

**Answer framework:** The constraint, your plan, your choices, the result.

**Example answer:** "Two days before a client launch, our translator fell ill. I listed what was essential, asked a bilingual colleague to check the key pages, and moved the remaining pages to a second release. The launch went ahead on time with the essential content, and the rest followed three days later."

**Common mistakes:** Saying you worked all night without explaining choices.

**Beginner adaptation:** Use an assignment or placement deadline.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Show triage and stakeholder communication.

### Q23. Tell me about a time you handled criticism.

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Openness to feedback.

**Answer framework:** What was said, your first reaction honestly, what you changed, result.

**Example answer:** "My tutor said my lab reports were accurate but hard to follow. I was annoyed at first, then asked for an example of a clear report. I changed my structure to put the result first. My next mark rose a grade, and I still use that layout."

**Common mistakes:** Claiming you never received criticism.

**Beginner adaptation:** Use tutor or coach feedback.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Use feedback from a senior or a review, and show durable change.

### Q24. Tell me about a time you went above and beyond.

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Initiative and ownership.

**Answer framework:** The need no one assigned, what you did, impact.

**Example answer:** "Our shop's returns were handled on paper, and customers waited. In my own time I built a one-page form and trained the evening staff. Return time dropped from about ten minutes to five on the days I timed. My manager adopted the form in the other branches."

**Common mistakes:** Describing normal duties as extra.

**Beginner adaptation:** A volunteer, society or classroom initiative.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Show scale and sustained effect.

### Q25. Tell me about a mistake you made and how you fixed it.

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Integrity and problem handling.

**Answer framework:** What happened, how you told people, fix, prevention.

**Example answer:** "I sent a payroll file with an incorrect rate for two staff. I noticed it an hour later, told my manager at once, and corrected the payments before they were issued. I then added a second-person check on rate changes, and none have been wrong since."

**Common mistakes:** Hiding the mistake, or minimising it.

**Beginner adaptation:** Use a small error with a clear fix.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Show disclosure to stakeholders and system change.

### Q26. Tell me about a time you had to learn something quickly.

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Learning agility.

**Answer framework:** The need, your learning method, speed, outcome.

**Example answer:** "Our team changed to a new project tool on a Monday, and I was the only one with a free week. I used the vendor's tutorials, wrote a one-page guide and ran a thirty-minute session on Wednesday. By Friday the team had moved their tasks over."

**Common mistakes:** Not saying how you learned.

**Beginner adaptation:** Learning a software, language or lab method for a course.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Show you learned and also taught others.

### Q27. Tell me about a time you managed multiple priorities.

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Organisation and judgement.

**Answer framework:** List, rank by impact and deadline, communicate, deliver.

**Example answer:** "During exam month I also had two shifts and a society event. I listed everything by deadline and importance, told the society I could only organise logistics, and asked my manager to shift one shift. I passed every exam and the event ran."

**Common mistakes:** Saying you "just multitask".

**Beginner adaptation:** Combine study with work or activities.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Show you said no, delegated or renegotiated.

### Q28. Tell me about a time you showed initiative.

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Whether you act without being told.

**Answer framework:** Spot the opportunity, take a small step, show result.

**Example answer:** "I noticed our clinic's FAQ list was out of date and patients asked the same questions at reception. I asked the manager if I could update it, drafted it with a nurse, and printed copies. Reception reported fewer repeat questions in the following month."

**Common mistakes:** Choosing an initiative with no result.

**Beginner adaptation:** Volunteer or campus examples.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Examples that affected the team or budget.

### Q29. Tell me about a difficult customer or client.

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Composure and service.

**Answer framework:** Listen, acknowledge, solve, follow up.

**Example answer:** "A guest was angry that her room was not ready. I listened without interrupting, apologised, offered a seat in the lounge with a drink, and checked housekeeping. I updated her every ten minutes and gave her a late checkout. She thanked me when she left."

**Common mistakes:** Blaming the customer.

**Beginner adaptation:** Retail, hospitality, or help desk experience.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Show de-escalation and a systemic fix.

### Q30. Tell me about a time you adapted to change.

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Flexibility.

**Answer framework:** The change, your reaction, steps, outcome.

**Example answer:** "When our department merged with another, my reporting line changed and half my tasks moved. I met my new manager, wrote down what I would keep and what I would hand over, and offered to document the old process. The handover finished in three weeks."

**Common mistakes:** Complaining about the change.

**Beginner adaptation:** Changing course, group or placement.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Restructures, systems, leadership change.

### Q31. Tell me about a time you worked under pressure.

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Resilience.

**Answer framework:** Pressure source, how you kept quality, what you learned.

**Example answer:** "On a busy Friday our kitchen lost a cook mid-service. I took over the grill, asked the runner to call out orders in sequence, and kept a note of delays for the manager. We served everyone, though some waited fifteen minutes longer than usual."

**Common mistakes:** Claiming pressure never affects you.

**Beginner adaptation:** Exams, shifts, live events.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Crisis responsibilities and decisions.

### Q32. Tell me about a time you persuaded someone.

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Influence without authority.

**Answer framework:** Understand their concern, use evidence, compromise, outcome.

**Example answer:** "I wanted our lab to switch to a shared sample register. The senior technician worried about extra work. I tested it for a week on my own samples and showed it saved twenty minutes of searching. He agreed to a trial and later made it standard."

**Common mistakes:** Pushing, or winning by authority.

**Beginner adaptation:** Convincing a group or tutor.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Persuading executives with data and risk.

### Q33. Tell me about a time you disagreed with a decision.

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Professionalism in dissent.

**Answer framework:** State concern respectfully, offer evidence, accept outcome.

**Example answer:** "My supervisor decided to ship an order with a minor print fault to meet a date. I raised the concern in private, suggested a partial shipment, and accepted her decision. Later the client complained, and she asked me to lead the reprint. I learned to document my concern in writing."

**Common mistakes:** Bad-mouthing the decision-maker.

**Beginner adaptation:** Group project disagreements.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Cross-functional or leadership disagreement and what you did after.

### Q34. Tell me about a time you made a decision without full information.

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Judgement under uncertainty.

**Answer framework:** What you knew, assumptions, decision, check.

**Example answer:** "A supplier said a part might not arrive on time. We had no certainty, so I ordered a smaller emergency batch from a second source, which cost slightly more. The first part arrived late, and the emergency batch kept production running."

**Common mistakes:** Saying you always wait for full data.

**Beginner adaptation:** Choice of project topic, tools or approach.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Budget, hiring or vendor decisions.

### Q35. Tell me about a time you took a risk.

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Appetite for risk and how you manage it.

**Answer framework:** Risk, safeguards, decision, outcome.

**Example answer:** "I proposed running our society's annual talk online for the first time. The risk was lower attendance. I tested the platform with a small group, kept a room booked as a fallback, and promoted it widely. We had more attendees than the previous year."

**Common mistakes:** Describing recklessness.

**Beginner adaptation:** Trying a new approach on a project.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** A business risk with mitigation and results.

### Q36. Tell me about a time you improved a process.

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Continuous improvement.

**Answer framework:** Problem, analysis, change, measure.

**Example answer:** "Our pharmacy team repeated allergy checks at two stages. I mapped the steps, saw the duplication, and proposed one verified check at intake. After a month's trial, queue times dropped, and the pharmacist approved it."

**Common mistakes:** Not measuring the change.

**Beginner adaptation:** A study or club process.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Cost, time or quality metrics.

### Q37. Tell me about a time you faced an ethical dilemma.

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Integrity.

**Answer framework:** The conflict, the principle, what you did, outcome.

**Example answer:** "A manager asked me to adjust a delivery date in our records so a target looked met. I explained that the record needed to be accurate, offered to show the real cause of the delay, and escalated to compliance when he insisted. The record stayed accurate."

**Common mistakes:** Choosing a minor dilemma or a fake one.

**Beginner adaptation:** Academic honesty, placement confidentiality.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Policy, compliance and escalation.

### Q38. Tell me about a time you worked with limited resources.

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Resourcefulness.

**Answer framework:** Constraint, priorities, creative options, result.

**Example answer:** "Our school club had a budget of 50 pounds for an open day. I borrowed display boards from the art department, asked members to bring baked goods, and printed posters at the library. We reached about 80 visitors."

**Common mistakes:** Complaining about constraints.

**Beginner adaptation:** Student societies and projects.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Budget cuts or headcount limits.

### Q39. Tell me about a time you had to say no.

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Boundaries and judgement.

**Answer framework:** The request, your reason, alternative offered, relationship preserved.

**Example answer:** "A colleague asked me to cover his shift on the day of my exam. I explained why I could not, and offered to cover the following week. He found another cover, and we agreed a swap."

**Common mistakes:** Never saying no, or refusing rudely.

**Beginner adaptation:** Balancing study and work.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Protecting scope and quality with stakeholders.

### Q40. Tell me about a time you explained something complex to a non-expert.

**What the interviewer wants to understand:** Communication clarity.

**Answer framework:** Know the audience, simplify with an analogy, check understanding.

**Example answer:** "I had to explain a billing error to an elderly customer. I drew a simple timeline of when charges applied, avoided jargon, and asked her to tell me back what she understood. She agreed with the correction and thanked me."

**Common mistakes:** Using jargon.

**Beginner adaptation:** Teaching a friend or presenting to a class.

**Experienced candidate adaptation:** Briefing executives or clients.
