Description (plain text):
Most money guides teach one skill and leave you guessing about the rest. The Practical Money System puts budgeting, an emergency fund, debt payoff, saving and the basics of investing into one clear order of steps you can follow at your own pace.

It is a 72-page educational guide for beginners, students, young professionals and freelancers. It explains each idea in plain language, shows the arithmetic, and gives you worksheets to fill in with your own numbers.

This is general financial education, not personal financial advice. It makes no promises about returns or results, and it does not recommend any product.

WHAT YOU GET
• 12 chapters, an introduction, a conclusion with a 90-day action plan, and four appendices (about 12,000 words)
• Three worked budgets: low income, middle income and irregular freelance income
• A monthly budget worksheet and an income and expense tracker layout, with advice for building it in a spreadsheet
• An emergency fund planner with a method for choosing a target, not a magic number
• A debt inventory and a debt repayment comparison worksheet
• A full comparison of three payoff strategies (avalanche, snowball and hybrid) on the same four debts, with the numbers shown
• A savings goal planner and a sinking-fund method for annual bills
• Compound interest explained with six worked examples, the rule of 72 with its error shown, and the effect of fees and inflation
• An introduction to investing and risk: volatility, diversification, time horizon, fees and common behaviour mistakes, with no product recommendations
• A scam-check checklist and what to do if you have been scammed
• A net worth statement, an annual review, a monthly review checklist and a week-by-week 90-day action plan
• A glossary and a reference list of official sources

FAQ
Q: Is this financial advice?
A: No. It is general education. It does not know your situation and cannot replace a qualified adviser.

Q: Will it work in my country?
A: The principles do. The examples use a neutral currency. Country-specific rules (tax, retirement accounts, consumer credit) differ, so the book shows you how to check your own.

Q: Does it tell me what to invest in?
A: No. It explains risk, diversification and fees in general terms and recommends no securities, funds or apps.
