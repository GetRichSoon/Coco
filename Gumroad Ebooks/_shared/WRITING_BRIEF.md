# Shared writing brief for Gumroad ebooks 02-05 (read fully before writing)

Project: five original, premium nonfiction ebooks sold on Gumroad. You write ONE complete manuscript. Date: 2026-11-07.
Owner's standard: genuinely valuable, practical, deeply organised, competitive with good commercial nonfiction. NOT short, NOT generic AI filler, NOT padded. Every paragraph must teach, instruct or give a usable template/example.

## Process (do all of it)
1. Read the existing competitor research for your book in `C:\Users\Lenovo\Good Space\Coco\Gumroad Ebooks\Organic Research\` (your book's file). Then do an extra research pass with WebSearch/WebFetch (standard mode): (a) read at least 5 more competitor product pages or descriptions where reachable; (b) look for buyer complaints and missing-information themes in reviews, Reddit threads or forum discussions about this topic (summarise what people complain about, in your own words, no quotes longer than a short phrase); (c) find official / authoritative sources to support factual statements. Write `<book folder>\research_notes.md` with dated (2026-11-07) findings, the complaints/gaps found and how the book addresses them, and a source list with URLs. If a page cannot be opened (403 etc.), say so; never pretend you read it.
2. Plan the differentiation: what will make this book better than the competition (use the gaps found).
3. Write the complete manuscript (not an outline).
4. Self-check (see end) and report.

## Hard rules
- Original text only. Do not copy other books, do not reproduce copyrighted passages, do not imitate another author's distinctive voice, do not copy competitor wording.
- No invented statistics, studies, quotes, testimonials, income numbers or case results. Any number must be (a) your own arithmetic shown in the text, or (b) from a source you opened, cited with link and date. Case studies/examples must be clearly marked "illustrative" or "fictional".
- No guarantees of income, employment, wealth, sales or results. No "get rich", no hype.
- Professional international English, clear, no motivational filler, no repetition, no generic AI-style paragraphs ("In today's fast-paced world", "unlock", "game-changer", "delve", etc.). Specific beats vague.
- Do not state anything as tested/verified unless you actually did it.
- Do not create images. Do not publish anything. Do not touch files outside your own book folder (except reading research and writing the one file named in your brief). No author name anywhere (none provided; do not invent one).
- No dash in the book title text.

## Word count
Target 8,000-12,000 meaningful words of manuscript text (your book's brief may allow more). Do not pad. Count with a script (words excluding markdown symbols; code blocks count as words) and report the number.

## Markdown conventions (a build script converts your files to a 6x9 in PDF)
- Write the manuscript as several files in your book folder: `manuscript_part1.md`, `manuscript_part2.md`, ... (each roughly 3,000-6,000 words so each file is writable in one go). Together they form the whole book in order.
- Do NOT write a title page, copyright page, disclaimer page or table of contents (the builder adds them). Do start with `# Introduction` (what the book is, who it is for, how to use it, what it does not do, a short "how this book was researched" note with the date 2026-11-07).
- `# ` (H1) = chapters, "Introduction", "Conclusion", appendices. Chapters: `# Chapter 1. Title`. Appendices: `# Appendix A. Title`. Use H1 ONLY for these. A page break happens before each H1.
- `## ` = sections. `### ` = sub-sections (use for numbered templates/questions/projects if helpful).
- Put anything the reader should copy (templates, scripts, prompts, code, worksheet text) in fenced code blocks (``` ). Keep code-fence lines under about 72 characters so they fit a 6 inch page (they wrap, but short is better).
- Tables: pipe tables, keep to 5 columns max, short cells. Checklists: `- [ ] item`. A blank line is REQUIRED before every list and table.
- Bold label lines like `**Problem:** text` are fine. No raw HTML, no emoji, no images, no footnote syntax.
- End with a source/reference appendix: `# Appendix X. References and further reading` listing only sources you actually opened, with URL and "accessed 2026-11-07", plus a one-line note on what each supports. Do not list sources you did not open.
- Include a glossary appendix where the brief asks for one.

## Required front-of-book content you DO write
- Introduction (as above). Conclusion at the end (a useful one: what to do next, a short action list).
- A "Common mistakes and fixes" part wherever practical, practical examples, step-by-step instructions, checklists, worksheets/templates as specified, an implementation roadmap.

## Self-check before you finish
- Run a word count script; grep your files to verify every numeric requirement in the brief (e.g. exactly 100 questions, 25 projects, 20 templates) and list the counts.
- Re-read two random sections for filler and fix.
- Final reply (max 12 lines): files written, word count, requirement counts verified (with the numbers), what you could not verify, anything the owner must decide.
